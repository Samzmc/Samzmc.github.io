Research Interests

Problems I want to understand

Evaluating language models

Whether an evaluation result means what it appears to mean: agreement with human judgement, score calibration, and the controls that separate a real signal from what the same measurement would show by chance.

Interpretability

Linear probes and truth representations, layer-wise and circuit-level analysis, and replication: which published effects survive a controlled design, and which turn out to be artefacts of the setup.

LLM agents and reasoning

Tool use through structured, inspectable interfaces, and how routing and deferral decisions change what an agent system actually gets right.

Models under deployment constraints

Small and compressed models in real settings: what compression, escalation to a larger model, and a small labelling budget cost in accuracy, and how to measure it honestly.

Research and applied AI

University of Cambridge, Department of Computer Science and Technology - Summer 2026

Research intern (UROP)

Eight weeks in the Natural Language and Information Processing group and the ALTA Institute, implementing and running the experiments behind two projects on language-model reliability.

Truth representations. Reproduced a NeurIPS 2025 interpretability result in PyTorch, audited the original pipeline and found eight design and implementation problems, then rebuilt the experiment as a 4,992-prompt controlled design on Llama-3.1-8B and 70B. About 45% of the published effect came from surface confounds. The same ablation reversed the conclusion between a constrained and a fully trainable model, which suggests that high probe accuracy can be an artefact of the architecture rather than evidence of a truth-tracking mechanism.

Small models as judges. Led the software, validation and analysis for a pipeline that scored 850 essays and 3,363 LegalBench items with eight open-weight models from 0.8B to 27B parameters, on A100 GPUs and hosted APIs. Aligning scores to 60 human labels fixed the score scale but not the ranking; measured against 500 random strategies that deferred exactly as many items, uncertainty-based routing held up in 37 of 40 legal conditions and none of 28 essay ones.

Selected Projects

Engineering work

Reproducing pico-decoder-tiny

Learning dynamics

Trained an 11M-parameter decoder-only language model (12 layers, grouped-query attention) from scratch for 2,000 steps on a single 16GB RTX 5080, with the Pico framework (Diehl Martinez et al., EMNLP 2025 demo). Training kept overflowing the card until I traced it to a silent Flash Attention fallback - a head dimension of 8, below the 16 the kernel requires - and restored O(n) attention memory with manual grouped-query expansion and a memory-efficient backend, bringing peak usage to about 11GB.

Analysing the checkpoints with CKA, Frobenius norm and proportional effective rank showed representations converging bottom-up: layer 0 is already above 0.88 CKA at initialisation, while layer 6 reaches 0.95 only at the end of training. MLP weights grew by up to 27% with their spectral structure unchanged from initialisation.

The project is motivated by a simple question: which parts of a compact language model change most during training, and how can those changes be measured beyond loss curves? I worked through it before applying to the NLIP group, several of whose members wrote the framework.
