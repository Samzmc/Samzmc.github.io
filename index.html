Research interests

Evaluating language models

Whether an evaluation result means what it appears to mean: agreement with human judgement, score calibration, and the controls that separate a real signal from what the same measurement would show by chance.

Interpretability

Linear probes and truth representations, layer-wise and circuit-level analysis, and replication: which published effects survive a controlled design, and which are artefacts of the setup.

LLM agents and reasoning

Tool use through structured, inspectable interfaces, and how routing and deferral decisions change what an agent system actually gets right.

Models under deployment constraints

Small and compressed models in real settings: what compression, escalation to a larger model and a small labelling budget cost in accuracy, and how to measure it honestly.

Department of Computer Science and Technology

Jun – Aug 2026

University of Cambridge · Research Intern (UROP), NLIP Group and ALTA Institute

Eight weeks implementing and running experiments for two projects on language-model reliability.

Truth representations. Reproduced a NeurIPS 2025 interpretability result in PyTorch, audited the original pipeline and found eight design and implementation problems, then rebuilt the experiment as a 4,992-prompt controlled design on Llama-3.1-8B and 70B. About 45% of the published effect came from surface confounds. The same ablation reversed the conclusion between a constrained and a fully trainable model, which suggests that high probe accuracy can be an artefact of the architecture rather than evidence of a truth-tracking mechanism.

Small models as judges. Led the software, validation and analysis for a pipeline that scored 850 essays and 3,363 LegalBench items with eight open-weight models from 0.8B to 27B parameters, on A100 GPUs and hosted APIs. Aligning scores to 60 human labels fixed the score scale but not the ranking; measured against 500 random strategies that deferred exactly as many items, uncertainty-based routing held up in 37 of 40 legal conditions and none of 28 essay ones.

Projects

Reproducing pico-decoder-tiny

Feb – Mar 2026

Independent project · Pico framework (Diehl Martinez et al., EMNLP 2025)

Trained an 11M-parameter decoder-only language model (12 layers, grouped-query attention) from scratch on a single 16 GB RTX 5080 laptop GPU; Paloma perplexity fell from 59,435 to 1,315 over 2,000 steps.

Peak memory sat at 15.5 GB of the card's 16 GB, and three changes to the attention kernels did not move it. The cause turned out to be two silent fallbacks: with a head dimension of 8, PyTorch skips the Flash and cuDNN kernels, and grouped-query attention then rules out the memory-efficient one too, leaving the O(n²) path. Expanding the K/V heads manually let the efficient kernel run and brought peak use under 11 GB.

Analysing the checkpoints with CKA, Frobenius norm and effective rank showed representations converging bottom-up: layer 0 is already at 0.88 CKA at initialisation, while layer 6 reaches 0.95 only at step 1,500. MLP weights grew by up to 27% with their spectral structure unchanged from initialisation.

Which parts of a compact language model change most during training, and how can those changes be measured beyond loss curves? I worked through this before applying to the NLIP group, several of whose members wrote the framework.
